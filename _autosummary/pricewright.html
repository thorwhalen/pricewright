pricewright¶

pricewright: price services and products from a model of their costs and effort.

Simple things simple:

>>> from pricewright import price, Rates
>>> from pricewright.specialties.training import talk
>>> q = price(talk(delivery="online"), Rates({"founder": 900}))
>>> q.unit_price > 0
True

A whole rate card, checked for consistency and compared with a market packet:

from pricewright import load_spec, build_card, render
print(render(build_card(load_spec("card.yaml"))))

Layers: base (data), model (the arithmetic), travel (on-site add-on), ratecard (card-wide checks), research (market packets), specialties (templates per kind of offering, in a registry), spec and report (YAML in, Markdown out).

pricewright.day_rate_from_income(target_annual_income, *, billable_days=110, annual_overhead=0.0, social_charges_rate=0.0)[source]¶

The day rate that pays a target income, given how many days are really billed.

A freelancer or a two-person studio bills far fewer days than it works: selling, admin, development and gaps eat the rest. billable_days is that real number, not 220.

>>> round(day_rate_from_income(60_000, billable_days=100, annual_overhead=10_000))
700

pricewright.position(value, comparables, *, metric='per_person_contact_hour', fx=None, currency='EUR')[source]¶

Locate value (already in metric units and currency) among comparables.

fx maps a currency to its value in currency (e.g. {"USD": 0.87} when currency='EUR'). A comparable in a currency missing from fx is skipped rather than silently treated as equal.

>>> cs = [Comparable(str(i), "training", "x", price=p, unit="per_person",
...                  contact_hours=1, evidence="fetched") for i, p in enumerate([40, 50, 60, 70, 80])]
>>> position(65, cs).verdict, position(65, cs).median
('within market', 60.0)
Return type:

Position | None

Modules

base

The data model of a price: who works how long, what it costs, and on what terms.

The cost-and-effort model: from an Offering to a price.

Consistency across a whole rate card.

Market research as an input: comparables packets, normalised and compared.

Rounding a computed price to a number a person would print on a rate card.
